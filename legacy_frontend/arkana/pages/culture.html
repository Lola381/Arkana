About

Warli painting is a form of tribal art mostly created by the tribal people from the North Sahyadri Range in Maharashtra, India. This traditional art form, characterized by its rudimentary graphic vocabulary, uses a circle, a triangle, and a square to represent the world.

Unlike other Indian folk art forms, Warli paintings do not depict mythological characters or images of deities. Instead, they portray the social life of the tribal people. Scenes of human figures engaged in hunting, dancing, sowing, and harvesting are common, reflecting a profound harmony with nature.

Origins in Antiquity

Rooted in ancient traditions, early forms of Warli art are believed to have originated in the Sahyadri ranges. The geometric motifs parallel prehistoric cave paintings.

10th Century CE

Establishment of Ritual Practice

The art form solidifies as a vital component of marriage ceremonies and harvest festivals, painted exclusively by the women of the tribe (Savasinis).

1970s

Commercial Introduction

Jivya Soma Mashe brings global recognition by painting on a daily basis for artistic expression rather than solely for rituals.

Present Day

Global Heritage

Warli motifs adorn modern fashion, interiors, and digital spaces. Efforts continue to preserve the authentic techniques and intellectual property of the indigenous creators.
